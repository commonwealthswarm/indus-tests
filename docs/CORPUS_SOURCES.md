# Corpus sources

Research notes only, checked 2026-10-16. No dataset or inscription sequence is reproduced here. Do not download, copy, convert, commit or post real inscriptions as part of this task, including small cited samples.

Counts below describe named editions or snapshots, not interchangeable totals. An object, a text, a line and a sign occurrence are different units. Availability, website copyright, book copyright and permission to reuse a dataset are also different. Quotes identify only the terms actually observed; missing permission is not replaced with an invented licence.

## indusscript.in (Mahadevan 1977 numbering)

- URL: [The Indus Script Web Application](https://indusscript.in/).
- Citation: Mahadevan, Iravatham, and Roja Muthiah Research Library (2021), *The Indus Script Web Application (IM77/IDF80)*. The [Indus Research Centre's own description](https://rmrl.in/en/irc) identifies the developer and the underlying 1977 book; the web-application citation also appears in the [data-availability section of Ansumali Mukhopadhyay (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02320-7).
- Sign numbering: Mahadevan's M77/IM77, with the digitized corpus identified as IDF80. Use `M77` for matching readings in this repository, and record the actual source revision separately.
- Approximate size: roughly 2,900 numbered texts and 417 main signs for the base M77 concordance, as reported in [Yadav et al. (2010), Materials and Methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0009506). This is a base-corpus reference, not a fresh count of the live application. The Night Shift aggregate-only `query_data(op="summary")` response on 2026-10-16 reported 2,906 text numbers, 3,916 stored text records, 13,372 tokens and 417 distinct signs for its attributed application snapshot. Those API field counts must not be relabelled as physical objects or printed lines.
- Terms/licence evidence: the [developer's website](https://rmrl.in/en/irc) says, "© 2026 Roja Muthiah Research Library. All rights reserved." This is a website notice, not a dataset licence. The application returned only its title through text retrieval; an attempted rendered-browser check was blocked by the local approval policy. No application-specific reuse terms were verified. Do not infer permission from public accessibility; confirm terms with the source before private acquisition or conversion.

## Mahadevan 1977 concordance

- URL: [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Indus_script.html?hl=en&id=7WkMAQAAMAAJ); [developer's description of its digital successor](https://rmrl.in/en/irc).
- Citation: Iravatham Mahadevan, *The Indus Script: Texts, Concordance and Tables*, Memoirs of the Archaeological Survey of India No. 77, New Delhi: Archaeological Survey of India, 1977.
- Sign numbering: `M77`. [Yadav et al. (2010)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0009506), coauthored by Mahadevan, reports 417 unique signs in 3,573 lines of 2,906 texts for the electronic concordance used in that study. These are the approximate size and units for that version, not a claim that every print/addendum/IDF80 variant has identical totals.
- Availability: the print concordance is a reference work; the machine-readable successor is the application above. The bibliographic record states "No eBook available"; that is an access statement, not a rights grant.
- Terms/licence evidence: no publisher-issued dataset licence or original-volume reuse terms were verified on the checked bibliographic and developer pages. The publisher's [ASI website](https://asi.nic.in/) could not be retrieved. This remains an explicit rights-evidence gap; no exact licence quotation is available to supply honestly. The PLOS article's "Creative Commons Attribution License" applies to that article and must not be assumed to license the separately published 1977 corpus. Obtain the applicable terms for one's own copy before conversion.

## Wells and Fuls, Interactive Corpus of Indus Texts (ICIT)

- URLs: [Current author-maintained entry page](https://www.epigraphica.de/indus/menueindus.htm); [older TU Berlin project page](https://www.user.tu-berlin.de/fuls/Homepage/indus/menueindus.htm).
- Citation: Bryan K. Wells and Andreas Fuls, *Interactive Corpus of Indus Texts*. Related books are Wells, *Epigraphic Approaches to Indus Writing*, Oxbow Books, 2011, ISBN 9781842179949 ([publisher](https://www.oxbowbooks.com/9781842179949/epigraphic-approaches-to-indus-writing/)); and Wells, *The Archaeology and Epigraphy of Indus Writing*, Archaeopress, 2015, ISBN 9781784910464 (print), 9781784910471 (digital) ([publisher](https://bcp.archaeopress.com/Archaeopress/Products/9781784910464)). These are distinct publications.
- Sign numbering: Wells/ICIT, updated by Fuls. `W2015` in [CORPUS_FORMAT.md](CORPUS_FORMAT.md) is an example label, not proof that a newer ICIT snapshot uses the unchanged 2015 inventory. Preserve the actual version; the 2022 and 2023 versions are specified below. Do not equate an ICIT identifier with an M77 identifier.
- Approximate size: Fuls's [2021 seminar abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351956125_Mathematical_epigraphy_and_the_Interactive_Corpus_of_Indus_Texts) reports 4,537 inscribed objects, 5,509 texts and 19,616 sign occurrences. This is dated evidence, not a live 2026 count.
- Exact access wording: the [current entry page](https://www.epigraphica.de/indus/menueindus.htm) says, "Ask the administrator (email: fuls(at)epigraphica.de) to get access to the database." The older page gives the TU Berlin address instead. These are access conditions, not an open-data licence. No explicit dataset redistribution licence was found on either entry page. A historical outage of the old host does not establish the status of the current service; authenticated database access was not tested.

## Fuls, Corpus of Indus Inscriptions and A Catalog of Indus Signs

- URLs and citations: Andreas Fuls, [*Corpus of Indus Inscriptions*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373522666_Corpus_of_Indus_Inscriptions), Mathematica Epigraphica 3, Berlin: independently published, 2022, ISBN 9781671804869; and [*A Catalog of Indus Signs*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373522673_A_Catalog_of_Indus_Signs), Mathematica Epigraphica 4, Berlin: independently published, 2023, ISBN 9798398422306. The [author's series page](https://www.epigraphica.de/mathematica/menueme.htm) also lists a 2023 second edition of the *Corpus*. The counts below identify the editions actually checked.
- Sign numbering: updated Wells/ICIT, not an unrelated Fuls numbering system. The 2022 *Corpus*, sections 2.1 and 2.5, uses May 2022 version 2.8, 709 signs, with three-digit identifiers and gaps in the numbering. The 2023 *Catalog*, section 1.1, uses May 2023 version 2.9, 713 signs; its contents list concordances with Mahadevan and Parpola. Use an edition-specific label and an explicitly verified crosswalk when comparing inventories.
- Approximate sizes: the 2022 *Corpus*, table 1.1 and section 2.5, reports 4,660 artefacts, 5,644 texts and 19,831 sign occurrences, of which 17,957 are legible. The 2023 *Catalog*, section 1.1, reports 4,674 artefacts, 5,659 texts and 19,869 sign occurrences. These overlapping ICIT editions are not independent datasets to pool.
- Exact rights wording: both author-uploaded books' copyright pages state, "All rights preserved." The notices identify Andreas Fuls and the respective years 2022 and 2023. This wording is transcribed as printed. It is a book copyright statement, not an open licence for either the online database or its underlying photographs. Only front matter, source descriptions and aggregate metadata were consulted; no inscription rows or sign lists are included here.

## Running privately

See [PRIVATE_RUNS.md](PRIVATE_RUNS.md) for the repository boundary and [CORPUS_FORMAT.md](CORPUS_FORMAT.md) for the schema. A user must resolve the applicable source permissions and use their own copy locally.

Keep a real corpus outside the repository. The existing `data/` and `*.private.json` ignore patterns provide additional local safeguards, but do not authorize publication. Convert locally into one JSON object containing `source`, `license`, `sign_list` and `inscriptions`. Each inscription needs a unique string `id`, a string or null `site`, and a non-empty list of string sign identifiers in `signs`. Record the source edition and actual rights evidence; do not assign an open licence merely because the source can be viewed online.

Follow the repository's documented reading-order convention: the first element represents the rightmost sign. Check the source's display and direction conventions before conversion; handle exceptional or uncertain directions explicitly instead of silently reversing every record. Match the reading's sign inventory to the corpus version and verify any crosswalk locally. Never commit, post, paste into lab notes or upload the converted corpus or any real inscription sequence. Only synthetic fixtures belong in the public repository.
